Carry out a site-wide copy pass to remove em dashes ( — ) from all body copy, headings, and UI text, except where explicitly noted.
WHAT TO REMOVE
The em dash character ( — ). This is a long dash used as a stylistic break in sentences. It reads as AI-generated copy and must be replaced throughout the site.
WHAT TO KEEP

Hyphens ( - ) in compound words. Do not touch these. Examples that must remain unchanged: "tight-knit", "nine-hole", "award-winning", "first-home", "north-west", "short-stay", "build-ready", "two-bedroom", "three-bedroom", "four-bedroom", "six-star", "family-run", "walk-to-it", "heritage-listed", "year-round", "Van Diemen's Land", "cost-benefit", "ready-to-build", "one-point", "single-family", and similar.
Em dashes inside verbatim quoted content. Do not modify text inside quotation marks on the Press & Coverage cards. Specifically preserve exactly as-is:

The Mercury pull quote: "How a young restaurateur is reinventing dining in Stanley."
The Australian pull quote: "From historic buildings to a striking geological landmark, Stanley has diversions aplenty."
The SMH article headline: "This Aussie road trip is outstanding, even if my car isn't"
Any byline format like "— Jim Darby" or "— Bridget Clarke, The Mercury" on the press cards. These are standard editorial byline attribution and should stay.


En dashes ( – ) in ranges (e.g., "2–3 times", "$600–$800k"). Leave these as-is.

HOW TO REPLACE EM DASHES
For each em dash removed, replace with the most natural punctuation or restructure the sentence lightly:

If it introduces an aside or clarification: replace with a comma or enclose the aside in commas on both sides
If it introduces a new thought at the end of a sentence: replace with a full stop and new sentence
If it connects two independent thoughts: replace with a full stop or semicolon

Maintain grammatical correctness, natural flow, and the original meaning. Do not simply delete em dashes and leave awkward syntax.
EXAMPLES OF CORRECT REWRITES
Before: "Original home of the Van Diemen's Land Company — open daily with views across to The Nut."
After: "Original home of the Van Diemen's Land Company. Open daily with views across to The Nut."
Before: "Stanley — Tassie's Top Tourism Town, Gold Winner"
After: "Stanley: Tassie's Top Tourism Town, Gold Winner" (heading — use a colon here)
Before: "The JDR packages are offered as one option among many — particularly suited to buyers who prefer a turnkey outcome with fixed pricing and a single point of contact."
After: "The JDR packages are offered as one option among many, particularly suited to buyers who prefer a turnkey outcome with fixed pricing and a single point of contact."
Before: "It's different here — this place has a culture of its own."
After: "It's different here. This place has a culture of its own."
